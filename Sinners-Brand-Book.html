01The concept

Born
guilty.

Sinners is for people who enjoy the things they’re told to skip.

The identity deals neon colours that clash on purpose into the look of an old deck of cards: tarot, poker, tattoo flash. Hand-drawn letters that won’t sit still, and a hand of bold little icons, one for every sin. Every bad habit becomes something to show off.

One brand, two temptations. Him runs electric blue against acid lime. Her runs hot pink against the same lime. Lime is the thread that ties the two lines together.

Loud

Neon on neon. Colours that clash and stay clashed. If it can’t be seen from across the street, it isn’t finished.

Playful

Icons dealt from old tarot and playing cards: hearts, daggers, flames, one for every deadly sin. Scatter them, tilt them, play them.

Unapologetic

The voice winks. It never whispers and never explains itself. Short lines, big type, zero confessions.

02The archetype

Born to break the rules. Raised to enjoy it.

One character runs this brand: an outlaw with a sense of humour and a weakness for temptation.

Every colour, letter, shape and line in this book traces back to it. That’s why the neon clashes, the type shouts and the copy winks.

When a new call comes up, ask what the Outlaw would do. Then make it funnier. Then make it harder to resist.

50%
Primary · a.k.a. the Rebel

The Outlaw

Rules are a dare.

Sinners exists to break the “you shouldn’t” rules. Have the dessert. Buy the second one. Indulgence is the point, not the guilt.

30%
Secondary · the wink

The Jester

Sin, but make it fun.

The rebellion is never angry. Every sin is played for a wink, and the joke is on the rules, never on the customer.

20%
Accent · the pull

The Lover

Hard to resist.

Temptation, attraction, scent as seduction. Him and her, two lines that pull at each other. It’s why everything runs a little warm.

Who we are. Who we’re not.

A brand that tries to be everyone looks like no one. These are the doors we keep shut.

We are

  • The Outlaw

    Breaks the rules on purpose, and in public.

  • The Jester

    Breaks them for a laugh. Never mean, never bitter.

  • The Lover

    Breaks them because it’s tempting. Scent, colour, a little danger.

We’re not

  • The Innocent

    Clean, pure, pastel. We’re not sorry and we’re not sweet.

  • The Ruler

    Gold foil, quiet serifs, luxury restraint. We’d rather be seen than whispered about.

  • Heritage

    Crests, sepia, “since 1887”. We borrow old card decks for their tricks, not their dust.

Vibration rule. Lime and pink are under 3:1 against each other, so small type buzzes. Set one on the other only at 48 px / 14 pt and up: headlines, the logo, big words on pack. Anything smaller on pink or lime, like body copy, labels and tags, goes in Sin Black.

Print note. Electric blue, hot pink and acid lime all sit outside the CMYK gamut, so a four-colour press will turn them dull. Print them as spot or fluorescent inks and approve each one against a physical Pantone guide on press. On screens, use the HEX values exactly.

07Graphic language

Pick a card.
Any sin.

Seven deadly sins and two wild cards, drawn like a tarot deck that’s been passed around a few too many bars.

Every icon is inked by hand: a wobbly Sin Black line, a Halo highlight and a hard shadow. Never ruler-straight, never perfectly smooth. Old playing cards and tattoo flash, turned up to neon.

Two wild cards

Every deck has a joker. Ours has two: the one who started it, and the thing he offered.

Small change

The four suits and a sparkle fill the gaps between icons. They’re confetti: never the hero, never bigger than a fingertip.

  • Hearts
  • Diamonds
  • Spades
  • Clubs
  • Sparkle

How they behave

  • Shadow falls down-right

    Every icon gets a hard offset shadow, about 4% of its size, toward the bottom right. In Sin Black, always.

  • Colour comes from the type

    Icons take the logo colour of their colourway. Halo, or Sin Black on Type 02, is the second fill. They never borrow from the other line.

  • Deal, don’t throw

    Tilt up to about 25° either way so a crown still reads as a crown. Mix sizes up to about 1:1.5.

  • Never under the logo

    Scatter around the wordmark and keep its clear space empty. On packaging, icons live along the top and bottom bands.

08Backgrounds

Three
patterns.

The designs from your references, rebuilt as repeatable brand backgrounds in every type.

All of them ship as seamless SVG tiles and high-resolution PNGs in the kit. Use one background per surface. Never layer two.

Deck scatter

The main background. Loud for outer packaging and campaigns. Tone-on-tone for box interiors, tissue and anywhere copy needs to sit on top.

The serpent

Thick looping lines with an offset shadow: the snake from the garden. Keep the loops at the edges and leave the middle open for the logo.

Temptation burst

Hard rays in two flat colours, inked at the edges, with a clean centre for the logo or the product. Loud like an old fairground poster. Good for bags, screens and single-product posters.

10Voice

No saints
allowed.

We talk like a friend daring you to do it anyway. Short, cheeky, sure of itself.

Do

  • Keep lines to five words or fewer.
  • Wink at indulgence: treats, splurges, staying out late.
  • Let the type be big and the words be few.
  • Talk to one person, like a dare.

Don’t

  • Use real religious symbols or poke at anyone’s faith. The sins are a joke about indulgence, not about belief.
  • Apologise, explain or over-promise.
  • Write long paragraphs on pack.
  • Swear. Cheeky beats crude.
